How to play

Solo Living Diary is a life simulation that follows your household finances one year at a time, from the day you start your first job and move out of your parents' home until your early 30s. The only decisions you make are whether to buy something or skip it, and whether to go High, Mid or Low. At the end, you choose to "Get married" or "Go on solo". In English, you choose where to play at the start: the United States (US dollars), Singapore (Singapore dollars), the Philippines (Philippine pesos), Canada (Canadian dollars) or Australia (Australian dollars). Rents, prices and incomes are based on each country's statistics. The amounts on this page are for the United States; see "Playing in Singapore", "Playing in the Philippines", "Playing in Canada" and "Playing in Australia" for what changes there.

What you decide at the start

  • Country or region: the United States, Singapore, the Philippines, Canada or Australia.
  • Gender: choose "Female" or "Male".
  • Age when you move out: 22 to 28.
  • Savings: $0 to $30,000, in steps of $1,000.
  • Annual income: your gross annual income. The default is the median income for your starting age. If you change the age, an income you haven't edited by hand is updated to the median for the new age.

Every item has a "Random" button, and "Randomize all and start" picks everything at random and starts the game right away.

How a year goes by

Once the game starts, it moves forward one year at a time on its own. You can switch the pace with "1x", "2x" and "Pause". When an event comes up, the game stops and opens a choice screen. Each button shows the cost and what your savings will be after you choose it ("→ Savings $…").

At the end of each year, that year's income and spending are calculated, your savings go up or down, and a "Year end" line is written in the diary. A breakdown of the year-end finances appears at the bottom of the screen for a few seconds (you can turn it off in Settings).

Playing in Singapore

The rules are the same, but the amounts follow Singapore's prices. Most young people who rent on their own take a room in a shared flat, and cars cost far more because of the COE.

  • Start: savings S$0 to S$30,000 (default S$10,000). The default income is S$33,800 at 22, rising to S$59,800 at 30. Take-home pay is 78% (after your CPF contribution and income tax).
  • Finding a place: Condo studio S$29,400 a year, move-in costs S$2,700 / Common room in an HDB flat S$12,000 a year, S$1,100 / Room in an older flat, far out S$9,000 a year, S$800. Every 2 years you renew (no fee, rent +3%) or move.
  • Living costs: S$5,000 a year. Eating habits: S$18,000 / S$7,000 / S$5,500 a year. Socializing: S$3,000 / S$1,800 / S$600. Hobbies: S$4,500 / S$2,000 / S$500.
  • Furniture (rooms usually come furnished): S$6,000 / S$2,000 / S$600. Investing in yourself: part-time degree modules S$4,000 / short course S$1,500 / self-study S$300.
  • Car: new S$200,000 (upkeep S$12,000 a year) / used S$110,000 (S$11,500) / cheap used S$60,000 (S$11,000). Travel: Japan or Europe S$3,500 / regional trip S$800 / day trip S$100.
  • Dating: S$10,000 / S$5,000 / S$2,500 a year. Partner search: dating agency S$3,000 a year / paid app S$300 / free apps and SDN events S$0. Friend's wedding: S$400 / S$250 / S$100 (ang bao).
  • Interest on negative savings: 27% (credit card rate). Bankrupt below −S$40,000.
  • Rank: S S$160,000 or more, A S$100,000 or more, B S$60,000 or more, C S$0 or more. Incomes rise faster with age in Singapore, so the lines are higher than in the US. Cutting everything leaves you listless, and you miss those raises.

Playing in the Philippines

The game is set in Metro Manila. The rules are the same, but the amounts follow Philippine prices. Incomes rise more slowly with age than in the US or Singapore, so a cheap start goes a long way.

  • Start: savings ₱0 to ₱300,000 (default ₱100,000). The default income is ₱281,000 a year at 22 (including 13th-month pay), rising to ₱351,000 at 30. Take-home pay is 88% (after SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG and income tax).
  • Finding a place: Condo studio ₱180,000 a year, move-in costs ₱45,000 / Studio-type apartment ₱84,000 a year, ₱21,000 / Bedspace near work ₱40,000 a year, ₱7,000 (1 month advance + 2 months deposit). Every year you renew (no fee, rent +2%) or move.
  • Living costs: ₱55,000 a year. Eating habits: ₱150,000 / ₱70,000 / ₱40,000 a year. Socializing: ₱25,000 / ₱12,000 / ₱4,000. Hobbies: ₱40,000 / ₱15,000 / ₱4,000.
  • Appliances & furniture: ₱50,000 / ₱20,000 / ₱6,000. Investing in yourself: evening MBA classes ₱40,000 / online course ₱20,000 / self-study ₱3,000.
  • Car: new sedan ₱850,000 (upkeep ₱75,000 a year) / used ₱420,000 (₱60,000) / cheap used ₱200,000 (₱55,000). Travel: Japan or Korea ₱60,000 / domestic trip ₱15,000 / day trip ₱2,000.
  • Dating: ₱80,000 / ₱30,000 / ₱15,000 a year. Partner search: matchmaking service ₱30,000 a year / paid app ₱4,000 / free apps ₱0. Friend's wedding: ₱15,000 / ₱5,000 / ₱2,000.
  • Interest on negative savings: 36% (the credit card ceiling). Bankrupt below −₱350,000.
  • Rank: S ₱800,000 or more, A ₱500,000 or more, B ₱300,000 or more, C ₱0 or more.

Playing in Canada

The rules and rank lines are the same as in the United States, but the amounts follow Canadian prices. Incomes rise faster with age, and rent increases are capped by provincial guidelines.

  • Start: savings C$0 to C$30,000 (default C$10,000). The default income is C$42,000 at 22, rising to C$64,000 at 30. Take-home pay is 77% (after federal and provincial tax, CPP and EI).
  • Finding a place: Newer 1-bedroom downtown C$31,200 a year, move-in costs C$3,500 / Average 1-bedroom C$20,000 a year, C$2,300 / Basement apartment farther out C$14,000 a year, C$1,500. Every year you renew (no fee, rent +2.1%) or move.
  • Living costs: C$6,000 a year. Eating habits: C$11,000 / C$6,500 / C$4,000 a year. Socializing: C$2,000 / C$1,300 / C$500. Hobbies: C$4,000 / C$2,000 / C$800.
  • Appliances & furniture: C$10,000 / C$4,000 / C$1,500. Investing in yourself: part-time master's courses C$5,000 / continuing-education certificate C$2,000 / self-study C$300.
  • Car: new C$45,000 (upkeep C$6,000 a year) / used C$25,000 (C$5,500) / cheap used C$8,000 (C$5,000). Travel: Europe or Japan C$6,000 / trip within Canada C$2,400 / day trip C$200.
  • Dating: C$5,000 / C$2,500 / C$1,500 a year. Partner search: matchmaking agency C$6,000 a year / paid app C$400 / free apps C$0. Friend's wedding: C$1,500 / C$600 / C$200.
  • Illness or injury: C$500–C$3,000 (hospital care is public; this is drugs, dental and lost wages). Interest on negative savings: 21%. Bankrupt below −C$50,000.

Playing in Australia

The rules are the same, but the amounts follow Australian prices. Incomes are high from the start, and so are rents, which rise about 5% a year.

  • Start: savings A$0 to A$30,000 (default A$10,000). The default income is A$62,400 at 22, rising to A$88,000 at 30. Take-home pay is 79% (after income tax and the Medicare levy; super is paid on top by your employer).
  • Finding a place: New 1-bed in the inner city A$44,200 a year (A$850 a week), move-in costs A$6,600 / 1-bed unit in the suburbs A$28,000 a year, A$4,000 / Older studio further out A$21,800 a year, A$2,800 (bond and rent in advance included). Every year you renew (no fee, rent +5%) or move.
  • Living costs: A$7,500 a year. Eating habits: A$18,000 / A$8,500 / A$5,500 a year. Socialising: A$6,000 / A$2,500 / A$1,200. Hobbies: A$6,000 / A$2,500 / A$1,000.
  • Appliances & furniture: A$10,000 / A$4,000 / A$1,500. Investing in yourself: part-time graduate certificate A$8,000 / TAFE course A$3,000 / self-study A$300.
  • Car: new A$50,000 (upkeep A$8,000 a year) / used A$27,000 (A$6,000) / cheap used A$10,000 (A$4,000). Travel: Japan or Europe A$6,000 / Bali or a domestic trip A$2,000 / day trip A$100.
  • Dating: A$7,000 / A$3,500 / A$1,500 a year. Partner search: matchmaking agency A$5,000 a year / paid app A$400 / free apps A$0. Friend's wedding: A$2,800 / A$1,200 / A$500.
  • Illness or injury: A$200–A$3,000 (Medicare covers public hospitals; this is gap fees, an ambulance and unpaid leave). Interest on negative savings: 18%. Bankrupt below −A$75,000.
  • Rank: S A$120,000 or more, A A$75,000 or more, B A$45,000 or more, C A$0 or more. Incomes are higher than in the US, so the lines are 1.5 times higher.

Fulfillment

Fulfillment is a 0 to 100 score for how satisfied you are with your life, starting at 50. Each year, the values of your current room, eating habits, socializing and hobbies are added, and it naturally drops by 3. Trips, friends' weddings and noisy neighbors also raise or lower it. It's shown as a face icon at the top of the screen.

  • 😊 Fulfilled (70 or more): promotion chance +5%, chance to meet someone +5%.
  • 🙂 Okay (31 to 69): no effect.
  • 😞 Listless (30 or less): no promotions, no age-based income growth, illness or injury chance +5%, chance to meet someone −5%, breakup chance +10%.

Fulfillment doesn't count toward your rank, but it changes the name of your ending.

Meeting someone and breaking up

In years when you're single, the game checks at year end whether you meet someone. The chance is 10%, plus a socializing bonus (Go to everything +10%, In moderation +5%, Only when needed 0, Mostly decline −5%), the partner search bonus and the fulfillment bonus. In years when you're dating, you may break up, with a chance that depends on how much you spend on dating.

How the money is calculated each year

  • Take-home income: 75% of your annual income (after federal, state and payroll taxes). Your income rises every year in line with the median income growth for your age (but not in listless years).
  • Living costs: utilities, phone and internet, daily necessities and so on. Starts at $5,400 a year and rises 1.25% every year.
  • Rent, food, socializing, hobbies, dating, partner search and car upkeep: the amounts you chose are charged every year.
  • Interest: if your savings are negative at year end, the shortfall is treated as credit card debt and charged 22% interest.

Things that happen each year

  • Promotion: 8% base chance. +5% when fulfilled, +45% during the 2 years you attend classes. No promotions while listless. A promotion raises your income 10%.
  • Illness or injury: 5% base chance. +5% with "Low" eating habits, +5% when listless. Out-of-pocket medical bills of $1,500–$5,000.
  • Random events: each year there's a 20% chance of one of these: a job offer (if you accept, your income changes by −20% to +20%; it's more likely to go up while you attend classes), a bonus cut (take-home pay −10% that year), a windfall ($1,000–$10,000), or a friend's wedding (Attend + after-party / Attend / Skip it, send a gift).
  • Appliance breakdown: with a chance that depends on the appliances you chose, one item breaks and you replace it for 30% of the purchase price.
  • Car breakdown: with a chance that depends on the car you chose, repairs cost $1,000–$4,000.
  • Noisy neighbors: happens with a chance that depends on your room, and fulfillment drops 5. You choose to move out, or put up with it (fulfillment drops another 5).

How the game ends

The game ends when you make your choice in "What comes next". If your savings drop below −$50,000 along the way, you get the "Bankrupt Ending" right then.

Your rank is decided by your final net worth (savings + the value of your car): S $80,000 or more, A $50,000 or more, B $30,000 or more, C $0 or more, D less than $0 (debt left over). There are 6 endings, based on whether you marry or go solo and on your final fulfillment (plus the Bankrupt Ending).

Titles and achievements

You get one title based on how you chose. The conditions are checked from the top, and the first one that matches is your title.

  • Fandom Is Life: chose "All in" every time for hobbies & fandom (2+ times)
  • Self-Improver: did every self-investment offered (2+ times)
  • Show-off: chose "High" in 70%+ of choices
  • Master of Thrift: chose "Low" or skipped in 70%+ of choices
  • Steady Type: chose "Mid" in 70%+ of choices
  • Social Butterfly: chose "Go to everything" every time for socializing (2+ times)
  • Serial Mover: moved 3 or more times
  • Car-Free Life: chose never to own a car
  • Well-Balanced: none of the above

There are 12 achievements (Savings Machine, Rock Bottom, Home Sweet Home, Migratory Bird, Certified, Good Move, Meant to Be, Search Pays Off, Every Day Is Fun, Debt-Free, Unexpected Fortune, The Story Continues). You can check their conditions under "Endings".

Continue in Newlywed Diary

From the results screen, you can move on to the sister game, Newlywed Diary. It carries over your gender, age, savings, income and car from Solo Living Diary so you can start married life. With a married ending it appears as a large button, and with a solo ending as a small "What if you'd married?" button. It doesn't appear with the Bankrupt Ending.

Saving your progress

Your progress is saved in your browser at the end of every year, and you can pick up where you left off with "Continue" on the title screen. The endings, titles and achievements you've seen can be checked under "Endings".
